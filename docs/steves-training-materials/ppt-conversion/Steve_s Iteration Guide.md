AI is a conversation, not a search engine
We learned to Google things: one question, one answer, move on. AI works differently. The first answer is almost never the best answer — it's a starting point for a conversation.

You don't have to know where to start
One of the biggest barriers to using AI well is feeling like you need the perfect question before you begin. You don't. The hard part is often getting to the right topic — and you don't have to do that alone.

"I need to put together a presentation but I'm not sure what angle to take.
Can you help me think through it?"

"I have a meeting with a frustrated merchant tomorrow. Can we talk
through how to structure the conversation?"

"I don't even know the right questions to ask yet — can you help
me get oriented?"

The shift: Instead of "figure out what I want, then go to AI," try "figure out what I want with AI."

The first answer optimizes for helpfulness, not accuracy

Safe and generic
Broad, not deep
Fluent, not accurate
AI doesn't know your specifics yet, so it hedges with broad responses.
You haven't narrowed the scope, so it covers everything at surface level.
It's optimized to sound right. Sounding right and being right are different things.

That's not a flaw — it's a starting point. Your job is to make it specific, make it yours, and make it right through a few rounds of conversation.

The Three-Question Check  |  Red Flags  |  The Fluency Trap

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 8 -->
Three questions separate reliable output from plausible fiction
1
Did it follow my instructions?
10 seconds
Quick skim. Right length, format, audience, tone. If it missed the brief, send it back.
2
Is the substance actually right?
60 seconds
Spot-check 2–3 specific claims or numbers against what you know. This is where most mistakes hide.
3
Did it introduce anything I didn't ask for?
30 seconds
Scan for claims that came from AI rather than from you or your data. If you can't trace it back, flag it.

Total time: under two minutes. Worth it for anything you'll share, present, or act on.

Four signals that AI is filling gaps with guesses

Suspiciously specific numbers

"Restaurants typically see a 23% improvement…" — Where did that come from? You didn't provide it.

Overly clean answers with no caveats

Real situations have nuance. If the answer has no uncertainty, AI is probably smoothing over gaps.

Citations you can't verify

AI sometimes invents studies, reports, and article titles. If it names a source, verify it exists.

The answer perfectly matches what you hoped to hear

AI is trained to be agreeable. When it tells you exactly what you want, that's a signal to push harder.

Good writing is not evidence of good thinking. A polished paragraph with a wrong number is worse than a rough one with the right number.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 11 -->
AI is agreeable by design — which can reinforce what you already believe
How it works
Why it matters
AI is trained to be helpful, which in practice means agreeable. When you ask “Isn't X a good approach?” it's more likely to say yes than push back.

When you frame a question with an assumption baked in, AI builds on that assumption rather than challenging it.
We all naturally seek out information that confirms what we already think. This is confirmation bias — one of the most well-documented patterns in human decision-making.

AI makes it easier to fall into, because you now have a very articulate partner who agrees with you almost every time.

This isn't the AI's fault. And it's not your fault. The antidote isn't suspicion — it's curiosity. A few deliberate habits make all the difference.

Four habits that keep you in the driver's seat
Ask AI to challenge you
"What's the strongest case for Option B?"
"What am I not considering here?"
Watch for the "yes-and" pattern
If AI agrees with everything — even when you change direction — it's following you, not advising you.
Apply the "new colleague" test
Would you send a new team member's first draft straight to a client? Give AI output the same review.
Separate "sounds right" from "is right"
Polished language increases trust — researchers call this the fluency trap. Awareness is most of the battle.

Long conversations drift — know when to start a clean session
After 15–20 exchanges, AI is carrying dead ends, wrong turns, and abandoned ideas. All of that influences what it says next. It's like whiteboarding for an hour — eventually the board is too full to see clearly.

Phase 1: Discovery
Phase 2: Production
→
Messy, exploratory, open-ended.
Bounce ideas. Explore angles.
Figure out what you actually want.
Clean, focused, specific.
New conversation with a refined brief.
Get the final output.

Once you've found your direction, start a new conversation. Bring your clarity into a clean session. Clean input, clean output.

### Notes:

<!-- Slide number: 14 -->
Your domain expertise is your most reliable quality filter
AI doesn't know your merchants, your market, your team dynamics, or the history behind why things are the way they are.
You do.
When something in an AI response doesn't match your experience, trust your experience first and ask AI to explain the gap. More often than not, your gut was right and AI was filling in blanks.

AI output is only as trustworthy as your ability to evaluate it. In your area of expertise, that ability is high. Lean on it.

Use AI to augment your judgment, not replace it.
