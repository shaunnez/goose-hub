Why AI Feels Disappointing

THE EXPERIENCE
You ask a question. You get a generic answer.

THE REAL PROBLEM
AI isn't bad at thinking. It's bad at mind-reading.
It doesn't know your company, your industry, your deadlines, or what you've already tried. So it gives you something that sounds smart but isn't useful.
It has no idea who you are, what you need, or what "good" looks like for your situation. The fix isn't better AI. It's better context.

THE REACTION
"AI is overhyped." "It's just a fancy search engine." "I could have written that myself faster."

Think of It Like Hiring a Consultant

NO BRIEFING
WITH A PROPER BRIEF

You hire a consultant and say:
You brief the consultant first:
"Help me with my marketing strategy."
"We're B2B SaaS, $2M ARR, selling to mid-market CFOs. Q3 launch. Budget is $50K. Last campaign got 2% conversion."

They don't know your industry, budget, audience, timeline, brand voice, or what you've already tried.

They know your constraints, audience, history, and goals.
They deliver a generic playbook that could apply to any company.
They deliver a targeted plan you can execute next week.
You paid for something you can't use.
Same consultant. 10x better output. The brief made the difference.

CONTEXT ENGINEERING
A structured brief you write once and reuse every time you talk to AI

It tells AI who you are, what you need, what "good" looks like, and what to avoid.
You paste it at the start of a conversation. AI instantly becomes a useful collaborator instead of a guessing machine.
Same AI. Dramatically better results. The context is the difference.

Your Profile: Tell AI Who You Are
ANSWER THESE 5 QUESTIONS
WHY THIS MATTERS

What's your role?
AI adjusts to your level
A VP gets executive summaries. An analyst gets detailed breakdowns. Same AI, different output.
"Product owner for the payments team"

What's your experience level?

Preferences prevent rework
"10 years in fintech, new to this company"
If you hate long paragraphs, say so once. You'll never get a wall of text again.

What tools do you use daily?

"Jira, Confluence, Figma, Slack"
PRO TIP

Be specific. "I'm a manager" is vague. "I manage a 5-person risk team covering APAC regulatory compliance" gives AI everything it needs.
How do you like information delivered?
"Bullet points first, details only if I ask"

What do you NOT want from AI?
"Don't give me fluff or corporate buzzwords"

SECTION 2

Your Goal: What Does "Done" Look Like?
ANSWER THESE QUESTIONS
THE SINGLE BIGGEST LEVER

What are you trying to accomplish?
Vague goal
"Help me with the risk assessment"
"Write a vendor risk assessment for the board"
AI produces a generic template. You rewrite 80% of it.

Why does it matter? Who benefits?

Clear goal with "done" defined
"Board needs it for Q3 compliance review"
"Write a vendor risk brief for the board. 2 pages. Risk scores for 4 vendors. Due Friday."

What does success look like?
AI produces something you can edit, not rewrite.
"A 2-page brief with risk scores and recommendations"

KEY INSIGHT
What's the timeline?
Most people tell AI what they want to DO but not what SUCCESS looks like. Defining "done" is the single biggest improvement you can make.
"Draft by Wednesday, final by Friday"

What have you already tried?
"Started from last quarter's template but the format changed"

Your Rules: Guardrails for AI
ALWAYS DO
DEPARTMENT EXAMPLES

Cite sources
Legal
"If you reference a statistic, tell me where it came from"
"Always flag potential liability. Never give definitive legal advice. Use 'may' not 'will.'"

Use our terminology

Marketing
"We say 'associates' not 'employees,' 'guests' not 'customers'"
"Match our brand voice guide. Never use exclamation marks. Keep sentences under 20 words."

Ask before assuming
"If you're unsure about my intent, ask instead of guessing"

Risk
NEVER DO
"Quantify risk with probability and impact. Never minimize findings. Use our 5-level rating scale."

Make up facts

"If you don't know, say 'I don't know' — do not fabricate"
WHY RULES MATTER
Without rules, AI optimizes for "sounds good." With rules, it optimizes for YOUR definition of good.

Use jargon my audience won't understand
"This is for the board. Write for smart non-specialists."

What You Know: The Briefing
WHAT TO INCLUDE
YOU KNOW MORE THAN YOU THINK

Facts and data you already have
Documents you could reference
You don't need to share them. Just describe what exists: "I have a competitor analysis from January."
"Q2 revenue was $4.2M. Churn is 8%. NPS dropped 12 points."

People and their perspectives

What's worked before
"CFO wants cost cuts. VP Sales wants investment. Board wants both."
"Last time we did a launch, the email sequence outperformed social by 3x."

Constraints you're working within

What you're uncertain about
"Budget frozen. Headcount at cap. Must use existing vendors."
"I'm not sure if we need FDA approval for this claim."

Telling AI what you DON'T know is just as valuable as what you do.
What typically goes wrong
"Legal always pushes back on timeline claims. Finance rejects anything without ROI."

THE SHORTCUT
Think: "If I were briefing a smart new hire, what would they need to know to be useful by tomorrow?"

THE SECRET
Context engineering isn't about the AI. It's about clarity of thought.

Writing a context brief forces you to articulate what you actually need, what success looks like, and what constraints matter.
Most people have never written that down. They carry it in their heads. Then they wonder why AI (and colleagues) don't understand them.
The brief helps the AI. But it helps you more.

Making It a Habit
15 minutes today saves you hours every week
THE 2-MINUTE UPDATE CYCLE
WHERE TO KEEP IT

AI got something wrong?
A note in your notes app
Add a rule: "Never assume X" or "Always check Y first"
Apple Notes, OneNote, Notion, Google Keep. Anywhere you can copy-paste from.

Your situation changed?

Custom Instructions (if available)
Update the goal or constraints. Takes 30 seconds.
ChatGPT, Claude, and others let you set persistent context. Put your Profile + Rules there.

Something worked really well?
Note it: "When I said X, the output was exactly right."

A shared team brief
Your team can share rules and knowledge. "Our department's AI brief." Consistency across everyone.

New task, same domain?
Copy your brief, change the goal section. Profile and rules carry over.

THE COMPOUNDING EFFECT
